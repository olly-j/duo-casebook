# Brand evidence coverage, checked 15 October 2026

This is a historical reference, not a Duo case test or a ranking of companies. All 21 requested brands have at least one original editorial hands-on source in the JSON. Sources are ordered with direct material/drop evidence and foldable experience first, followed by fit and everyday-use reviews. This ordering is editorial, not a quality score.

`historical-hands-on` means a reviewer actually handled the named previous case. It does not mean that an independent laboratory validated durability, that every source was purchased independently, or that a Duo version performs similarly. Single-sample experience, subjective grip judgements and accidental drops are explicitly limited. Manufacturer drop-height claims repeated in reviews are not counted as observed drop results. No warranty implies protection of the enclosed phone.

`sponsored: null` means paid sponsorship could not be established from the article, not that sponsorship was ruled out. Manufacturer-supplied samples and affiliate links are noted in cautions. `independent-test` identifies original editorial testing rather than a vendor page; sample provision does not itself prove paid sponsorship. Null warranty/returns fields mean not verified in this workstream, not no policy. Policy dates are null where no publication date was established. Dates otherwise come from the visible article heading, not a search engine's relative age or crawl date.

Original review pages were opened and read using online search and browsing. Search snippets alone were not used as case-performance evidence. The Consumer Reports FineWoven page returned 403 when opened and was excluded. Retail listings, customer-service scores, vendor comparison blogs, unattributed synthetic-looking summaries and shopping roundups without specific first-hand findings were not used. The Case Tester TORRO comparison was retained because the author says the cases were purchased and supplies model-specific observations; its publication date and long-term wear method were not established.

Coverage is deliberately compact: usually one strong source per brand, with a second where it adds a materially different model or test. Apple returns and Mous warranty/returns were verified from manufacturer pages. Other policy fields remain null. No independent-test gaps remain at brand level, but most brands have no foldable test in this selection and none has a Duo case test here. Historical findings must remain labelled as historical in the finder.

Important limits: the Android Central BURGA/dbrand drop article uses one Pixel 8 and sequential tests, with an added screen protector. Its shopping widgets mention other models; evidence follows the article's stated test phone, not those widgets. The Mous iPhone 17 article inconsistently calls the range Limitless 5.0 and refers to multiple variants; use its reported findings without assigning them to every finish or revision. Jerad Hill's Spigen and SUPCASE reports contain detailed self-reported tests but lack a reproducible full protocol, raw records and a clear sample-funding disclosure. They are not certification or proof of a failure rate. The ZAGG article identifies iPhone 17 Pro/16 Pro together without cleanly separating every observation by handset.
